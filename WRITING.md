# Writing rules for the Chronicle

These rules come from the author's style guardrails for *On Jediism* (`Style Guardrails - AI Artifact Watchlist.md`) and apply to every chapter, interlude, and page on this site.

## Banned as a reflex: mirrored antithesis

The "not X, but Y" template, in all six disguises:

1. Same sentence: "not X, but Y" / "not just X, but also Y".
2. Sentence pair: "That does not excuse the errors. It means they are workable."
3. Reversed: "Y, not X".
4. Anaphoric mirror: "The question is never X. The question is Y."
5. Preposition skins: "Y rather than X", "instead of X, Y", "no X, only Y".
6. Double-negative parallel: "The teacher is not exempt from learning; the student is not raw material."

**Fix:** state the positive claim and let it stand. If the negation adds nothing, cut it.

**Exemptions:** doctrine quotes, comparisons of two genuinely different things, one earned aphorism per chapter at its climax, instructions to the reader.

## Other sentence-level bans

- **Superficial -ing tails.** "…, highlighting the risk", "…, ensuring safety". Cut if the tail carries no new fact.
- **Copula avoidance.** "Serves as", "stands as", "represents" where "is" works.
- **Significance inflation.** "Of utmost importance", "for good reason".
- **Vague attribution.** "Scholars say", "experts believe". Name the source or cut it.
- **Elegant variation.** Cycling synonyms for the same noun. Repeat the right word.
- **Nominalization pileups.** "The embodied experience of connecting with circumstance."
- **Hedge that undercuts.** Claim what the text earns.

## Voice rules

- Limit passive `to be` constructions. Prefer a human subject doing the verb.
- Keep subjects concrete. People act; weather happens; institutions do things to people.
- Let local voice carry bias. Rockside's people speak from where they stand.
- Second person for the intro/prose pieces, third person past for the Chronicle chapters.
- Concrete nouns over abstractions. One telling detail beats three adjectives.

## Pre-flight checklist

1. Search for `not … but`, `rather than`, `instead of`, `no … only`.
2. Search for `was/were …ed` passives and rewrite with an actor.
3. Search for `, …ing` tails at sentence ends.
4. Check that each paragraph has a body, a place, or an action in it.
5. Read the first sentence and the last sentence aloud. Both must stand alone.
