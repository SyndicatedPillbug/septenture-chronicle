<span class="dateline">Umbria, 1396cr · the old Vahael estate, the Whites</span>

# 14 — The Cistern House

Stross came back to the estate with a second captain: Selim Varek, white and gold, a believer, and an old ally with orders to protect the party and assist them. Page met him with suspicion. Ceridwen looked longer and found a man who had known loss and wanted to be useful in the face of it. When Legion explained that someone was stealing a sentient hive's larvae, Selim arrived at the first legal puzzle of his new assignment: if the hive is a citizen, the theft of its brood is kidnapping. The party went to grant citizenship to wasps.

They went over the wall into the old Vahael property, which had been noble once and was now a lesson in what happens to a house nobody lives in. Weeds, a dry fountain, statues face down in the grass. At the back stood the cistern house, its iron ribs green with age, its doors disturbed. One hinge had been melted shut with evocation. Something had been bolted across the entrance once and later removed.

Coy cast and found the whole place soaked in transmutation, spread through stone and soil like a stain. The signature felt like Legion and did not belong to Legion.

Inside, under green-brown light and vines, the old cistern ran dark and deep, with planter beds and cracked paths and a tunnel where the hive had made its home. Legion went in first with honey and peaceful intent. Page reached out along the mental link to say that they meant no harm. The hive had been smoked and drugged and robbed too many times to believe either of them. It came at the party as a living storm.

Legion did the only thing that could reach it. They let their shape go. The clay tablets and the little runes clattered to the floor, the robes collapsed, and the swarm that is Legion rose and joined the greater one. For a moment they nearly lost themselves in the other hive's fear and rage and confusion, in feelings too large for a mind still forming. Coy sent magic and a reminder to remember who they were. Ceridwen played the campfire song Legion had heard a hundred times on the road. Page held the link open and let them through.

Legion answered the hive with memory instead of argument: Simon alive, Simon dead, Simon helping, Simon gone.

The storm slowed. The swarms came apart. And in the middle of the cistern, the other hive began to take a shape — a person, a mirror of Legion at first, and then something beginning to be itself.
