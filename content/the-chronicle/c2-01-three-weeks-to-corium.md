# 2.1 — Three Weeks to Corium

They walked south for nearly three weeks, and nothing tried to kill them, which came as a novelty. The road took them through towns that had never heard of the Five and did not care about their medals, and the Five did small work for small money, and the war they had just survived began to feel like weather that had passed.

Corium announced itself by smell: salt, tar, fish, and the particular stink of a river meeting a harbor at low tide. The continent is enormous, and the party did the sums in an afternoon and chose the sea. A few weeks of water beat several months of walking, even with the price.

At the docks they found Captain Kellen Voss, who had one eye half shut at all times and hands that had done more rope work than they had ever done anything else. His ship, the Salt Mare's Fortune, carried twelve crew and a reinforced hull, which he mentioned twice and charged for once. Passage came to a hundred silver a head. Bill the mule would ride for free and eat on the party's account.

They were three hours in port and used all of them. The Mercenary Guild medallions opened the local outpost and let them draw against their writs, which is the only kind of banking where the collateral is reputation. Legion carried thirty bundles of straw aboard for Bill over the crew's objections. Coy found an apothecary. Simon bought things to keep his hands busy at sea, on the theory that idle hands aboard a ship find nautical trouble.

Page sold the leavings of the ambush. The merchant yawned at the common vials and offered a price on the brass calipers and weights that would have insulted a stranger. Page read him in one glance, told him a story about a family heirloom, and stood there smiling until the price improved.

The tide turned in the afternoon. They loaded the last crate, said goodbye to the road, and watched Corium slide away behind the ship's stern until it was just a smudge of smoke on the coastline.

Bill ate straw. The crew gave the party the sort of looks that crews give passengers who have brought a mule. The Salt Mare's Fortune leaned into the southbound current and put the coast out of sight by the second day.
