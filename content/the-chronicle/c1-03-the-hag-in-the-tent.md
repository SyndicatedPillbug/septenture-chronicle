<span class="dateline">Oxia 16th, 1396cr · the Feylight Festival</span>

# 1.3 — The Hag in the Tent

Coy went into his spider shape and webbed her before she finished the word. Ceridwen laid a hex on her and set the torches around the tent alight so she could not slip away into the dark. Legion's staff found her ribs twice. Page and Simon worked the edges, blade and magic.

She tore free of the webbing anyway and gathered herself to fight properly. Then she bled. Six orbs rose off her in a slow ring, each one glowing with the same crooked light, and they lured Legion in with a feeling he had not asked for. He touched one and caught fire, and the pain burned the charm out of his head. Page went down and Ceridwen brought him back up on his feet.

Coy ended it. He took the last of her with his spider eyes locked onto hers, and the blood orbs went on hovering until five tired people popped each one in turn.

The heap on the floor had been a woman, once. It was deformed now and marked by what had been done to it. Page searched it and found a coin pouch with fifteen silver and a dagger. Legion and Ceridwen searched the rest and found two things worth carrying out: a bracelet made of bones and carved with markings that gave off a palpable necromantic weight, and a small glass vial of iridescent liquid that glowed pale green.

A tall man came through the tent flap before they had settled their hands. Long black hair, red coat, black cravat, an angular face that gave away nothing. Page tried to explain and made a mess of it until Simon took the conversation and guided it to the hole in the perimeter.

He introduced himself as Barbello. He recognized Simon's name and the temple attached to it, and he owned the festival. The Five told him about the hag, and about Ethan, and he listened to all of it with the face of a man balancing books. He did not know the corpse. He knew the face of everyone who came into his festival, and this woman had never come in.

Ceridwen said the woman had probably died before someone carried her in, and Barbello found that interesting enough to ask about her fey knowledge. She told him her mother had liked stories, and let it sit there.

He offered work. Not through the guild. Four days north of the festival there were ruins, and if all went well the trip would be boring, and if it did not go well he wanted to hear about it quickly. He paid in silver tickets, ten of them, good for equipment at the exit, and let them keep what the hag and the corpse had carried. Legion asked about proper money and got a patient answer from a man who had stopped being impressed.

They took the job. Ceridwen would have gone alone if it came to that; the hag had the Coalmyr Gate written all over it and she wanted to know how. Simon sketched the corpse for the record. Barbello called Ceridwen back as they left, and Page stayed with her, and the two of them came out with a page of advice in Ceridwen's hand.
