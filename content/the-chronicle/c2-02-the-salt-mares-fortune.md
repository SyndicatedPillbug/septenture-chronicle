# 2.2 — The Salt Mare's Fortune

The voyage ran between nineteen and thirty days, depending on the weather. That was long enough to learn the crew's names, the cook's limitations, and how the night watch talked.

Ceridwen made a friend of the sailor Treza Jambad, who had stories and a willingness to spend them. The best of them was about the Gilded Hind, found adrift off the Norforth Coast three summers back. She looked perfect from the rail. Sails full, lanterns lit in broad daylight, not a scratch on her. Nobody on the ship had died of plague or pirates; the whole crew stood on the deck where they had been standing when it happened, frozen and bloodless with their mouths open and one arm raised, every one of them pointing at the empty crow's nest. The wizard who tried to read the death rolled his eyes back and wept black tar. They burned the Hind where she drifted. Treza said he still checked his own crow's nest on quiet watches, out of respect.

The crew argued about Danruian. Some had heard the island nation was massing an invasion fleet; older sailors dismissed it as the same rumor that surfaced every spring. Simon handed out gifts of tobacco and carved scrimshaw to the off-duty watch, earning quick friends across the forecastle. Page handed Coy and Legion an ink-stained foraging list for their first day ashore: owl feathers, licorice root, gum arabic, and a live spider.

On the day the coast came back, the wind dropped. The city rose out of the haze in layers, a cliff covered in buildings, the smoke of the Shave sitting over the top of it. The docks were closed. A blockade of writs and soldiers turned the ship away from the harbor and sent it along to a fishing beach to the east, where the passengers could walk.

So they walked. The cliff trail ran above the water for the last miles, past the Great Ox-Elevators descending into the rock with their cargo, and down to the inland gate. The gate was still taking travelers, and the queue moved at the speed of paperwork.

The talk in the queue carried the city's news past them while they waited. Murders in the Shave. People gone missing out of the cliffside warrens, year after year, with nobody counting them. Nobody in the queue said either thing above a mutter.

When their turn came, the guards looked at five tired travelers with a mule and found nothing worth writing down. Above the wall, the city climbed the cliff in steps, with a brown smear of refinery smog over its far end.
