# 1.7 — The Cellar Door

Legion came back from the woods with a pot of metallic blue wasps and a story about forceful diplomacy. Page thanked the wasps for their cooperation.

The mayor's house sat on a hill that had been built rather than found. Ceridwen got the rest out of Carly Two-Ton over a second breakfast: recent work in the mayor's cellar, a wife devoted to Alleviatira, and money that preferred not to be counted. The Five argued about sneaking in and decided against it.

Simon did the talking, which is what Simon is for. Wilherd Greyson turned out to be a young man in a good coat who liked the sound of his own hospitality, and by the time the tea was cold he had agreed to let them see the cellar. He also took the danger seriously enough to write to the Mercenary Guild and the local magistrate about it, which nobody had asked him to do.

The cellar held a circular stone door with elvish letters around its rim, older letters beneath the newer ones, both of them telling the reader to go home. Fiona produced a key that did not match the honesty of the moment: a master key from elvish royalty, which she had gotten from a guildmaster named Cormac and had not mentioned before now.

Page took that apart the way he takes apart a badly bound book. She had done seven relics for Cormac already. She was not in the guild and had not been for years, which Ceridwen caught her admitting when she said she wanted back in. Legion offered to settle the matter with steel, and the others held them off long enough for Fiona to keep talking.

She cried instead, which was worse, and told them why she had been thrown out. In Raifor she had watched a nobleman named Cedric Vardon kill a woman in his own house. She reported it to her captain, Veryn, and Veryn had her expelled for saying it, and told her she was lucky to be alive, and the only reason she still was came down to her father. Cormac had been using that ever since, promises in exchange for relics.

Coy asked whether the woman she saw might have been Beryl, who led the Firestarters before him. Fiona could not say. The question sat there and did not get answered.

Page pointed out that Cormac had taken seven relics and kept none of his promises, and that a person with her skills had other uses. Simon offered her a death. His family had ships. If Fiona died here, in a ruin, quietly, then Cedric Vardon's problem with her died too, and she could start again somewhere the guild had never heard of her.

She said yes.

Behind the door, the wailing started up again, and they let Fiona's funeral wait for later. Page carried the master key. The first passage ran long and low, with two doors off it and a larger one at the end. Simon lit the hallway with a stone and stood guard while the others worked the rooms. The north chamber was an embalming room with rites carved into the walls, the sort the old elves wrote before they learned to tolerate humans. Page took rubbings of the letters someone had tried to scrub away. The south room held pedestals with motes of light floating above them, and nothing Legion could provoke by throwing a pebble at one.

Then the wail came again, closer, like a metal bird crying, and Coy's stomach turned over. They put their hands on their weapons and went to meet it.
