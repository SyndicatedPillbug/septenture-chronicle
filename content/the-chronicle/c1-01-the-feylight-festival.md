# 1.1 — The Feylight Festival

Autumn sat heavy on the hills outside Lobridge, and the Faery Fire Five came down the road toward Crossroad with nothing much to do. Then the rumors found them: the Feylight Festival would open that evening, a day's walk from where they stood. Page had been to one before, years back, and said so. By dusk they had taken a room in a Lobridge inn and watched the colored lights come up over the trees.

The evening went wrong before it began. A couple in the street, Ernest and Elvira Copperfield, were calling their son's name. Ethan had been missing two hours, ten years old, and angry with them about the festival. Coy thought he saw the boy. He and Ceridwen followed the shape across town, cornered an old smith instead of a child, and talked him down when he thought they meant his granddaughter harm. Page and Simon stayed with the parents. Ernest arrived at the thought that undid him: he had brought Ethan a piece of fey clockwork from the city, and a boy with a toy like that might go looking for more.

Half the town was walking to the festival by then. The Five walked with them. On the road a weasel barred the way, backed by a dozen smaller weasels, furious at having been made to think and then charged no rent. Talking failed. Legion tried to go around, failed, and made peace by offering a dried rat from their rations. After that the weasel accepted tribute from travelers at one silver a head, and business was good.

Past the lantern-lit woods the path met a bridge, and the bridge was held by men who hated the fey. Ernest's story of a boy taken past the tree turned them aside. They let the Five through and fell to fighting each other moments later.

At the tree waited Thom, half man and half goat, who greeted them warmly until Ethan came up. The boy had slipped past the toll, which broke festival law and put him outside the feylands for good. The Copperfields could not enter. The Five could, for a gold piece each.

Ceridwen paid with a story instead. The tales of the festival belong to it forever, but the telling belongs to the teller, and hers moved Thom enough that he opened the whole gate for them.

Inside, the magic settled over the party like weather. Coy gave way to it. Legion shook it off and trusted nothing after that. Simon forgot the mission entirely and went shopping. Page wanted a bookshop.

He found one, and a fey called While who gave him the directions four left turns deep. The shop belonged to Kajik Buchift, an odd creature with double eyelids and a fine stock. Page traded a promise for a book, due back in a year. He also saw a horned woman across the aisle who looked at him with more interest than the afternoon required. Then he left to find his friends before he did anything about it.

Simon, meanwhile, had been led to a booth where a hammer and a bell settle all arguments. He rang the bell hard enough to empty his head and won a silver ticket for it.
