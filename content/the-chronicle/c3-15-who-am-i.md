# 15 — Who Am I

The new swarm-being stood in the green light and asked its first question. Not what are you, or why are you here. Who am I.

Legion turned it back on the creature, the way someone had once turned it back on them. When it admitted it did not know, Legion gave it the sunrise and the sunset, the going up and the going down, and the plain fact that not knowing is part of being alive. The room eased. The hive began to trust them.

It was afraid, it said. Something came at night and took its larvae. There were places in its own home it could no longer enter. It showed them the routes: the hive tunnel, a human-sized entrance the intruders used, and an old safe passage that had gone bad. Legion trapped the human entrance with caltrops and went to look at the bad passage, where they found a white powder that killed every wasp sent into it. Coy took one look and named it: a toxin built for swarm creatures, and nothing else. Ceridwen and Coy pulled three trap nodes out of the tunnel with dispelling magic, and Coy washed the poison out with shape water.

Page went into the hive tunnel to learn why the swarm could not simply leave. At the back, past the mud nests, he found an old petrified skeleton set into the stone, and the hive's living cord running straight into its open mouth. Inside the skull, when he looked, was a piece of mind stone. It glowed pale gold, the same color as the stones in the queen's vision.

The stone was the anchor, the core that sustained the swarm's sentience. The creature did not know whether carrying the relic would grant it mobility outside the nest, but recognized the item as its own property. Coy warned against returning it to the hollow skull, where the previous attackers had clearly known to search, so the swarm drew the stone into the center of its mass and tucked it into an inner comb.

Legion talked with it about travel and leaving and names. The hive understood that naming was not something a person did alone, and asked Legion to come back.

They returned to the estate to gather supplies and report, planning to keep watch that night. Ceridwen finally got a moment alone in her room, grieving and trying to breathe, and heard someone come in behind her a half-second too late. A gloved hand closed over her mouth.
