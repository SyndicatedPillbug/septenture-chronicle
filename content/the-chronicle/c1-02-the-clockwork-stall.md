<span class="dateline">Oxia 16th, 1396cr · the Feylight Festival</span>

# 1.2 — The Clockwork Stall

The bell had done its work on Simon. He came back to himself with a silver ticket in his hand and no idea where he stood, and the rest of the party spent the next hour getting their bearings. There is no logic to festival streets; there is only traffic. They went north with everyone else.

A thin drow found them in the crowd with a note from Page. The hand was hasty and the map worse, but the message was Page's: he had found something, and they should look for the clockwork stall. Legion paid the messenger with a field rat and Simon added five silver, because someone had to be respectable.

The next stall belonged to Alara, a pale full-blooded elf who sold jewelry and knives and never once looked bored. Her prices came from fey law. When Simon asked the way to Ethan, she named a memory as her fee, and he whispered one to her and did not say what it was. She took it and said it might matter later.

Then she pointed northwest, at a star bright as the moon, and told them the boy had to leave before dawn or he would belong to the feylands for good. Coy asked whether dawn was close. Alara answered with a fortune instead: he would find what he was looking for, and she did not necessarily mean the boy.

The clockwork stall stood under the star. A grey ogre worked at a tall bench behind a row of clocks, in a leather apron, alone with the pieces. He was having a bad day, and the reason was Ethan. The boy had broken something earlier, and the ogre had charged him a hundred years of service for it.

Ceridwen took over from there. She talked the ogre down to a payment in kind, one item of equal value to the thing broken. The ogre considered. He also ruled that Ethan and his family, and their children after them, would never enter the festival again. The knife they offered came from Alara's stall; the ogre knew her and disliked her, and took it anyway, saying it was worth more than they knew.

Ethan came out of the back room wild and ready to run, and only Simon's hand on his collar slowed him. The broken thing sat on the bench: a little brass globe, dented, its gears out of place. Simon looked at it a long moment and saw a map in its dents, larger than any he had seen. He set about memorizing it.

Coy bought a clockwork bird off the counter and gave it to the boy. Page, arriving at last with a book for Ethan under one arm, put it in his hands with the remark that curiosity should always get paid.

They walked Ethan back toward the gate, where the Copperfields and Thom waited. Ceridwen had written the parents a letter; Coy made the boy promise to deliver it, and to come find them when he had made something fine. Ethan jumped through to the mortal side. The Five turned back into the festival.

It was near midnight when Page brought them to the edge of the grounds. He had found a hole in the festival's perimeter, which should not exist, and the thing that made it had gone to ground in a tent with no show set up. They crossed to it and found Page's warning sign flat on the ground. Inside, in the dark, an old woman stood over a heap on the floor and said there had been an accident. She asked them, smiling, for help.

Legion and Ceridwen did not move. When Coy lit a flame, the woman's bones turned, her eyes went red, and claws slid out of her fingers. "There's no fooling the likes of you," she said.
