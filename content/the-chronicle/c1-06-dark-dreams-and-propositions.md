# 1.6 — Dark Dreams and Propositions

They slept late in two rented rooms, the way people do after a festival. Coy dreamed of a forest and a girl walking beside him, and then of an ocean, and then of going under. In the other room Ceridwen dreamed of standing at the edge of a black whirlpool with her friends' voices coming up out of it, and of reaching down to save them with hands that wanted to hurt instead.

She woke on the floor. Legion was already awake across the hall, trying to rouse Coy with a waterskin, and getting nowhere. When Ceridwen put a hand on Coy's forehead, his fever pulled her into the dream after him. She stood on water and hauled him back to the surface, and six figures watched them from the dark. A mark glowed on Coy's chest in the shape of the bracelet they had taken off the hag.

They came out of it with the bracelet stuck to Coy's skin and Ceridwen holding it in her bare hand. Page woke in the next room, knew something was wrong, and crossed the hall at a run with Simon behind him. What they found was Ceridwen standing, eyes open, caught in the dream with the bracelet fixed to her palm.

Page knew what it was. He slapped her cheek, told her to attune, and she heard him through the water. The six figures came close and spoke. A girl. A man. Then a woman with a queen's bearing who gave her name as Lilliana of Trey, who had fought a hag, lost her essence to it, and watched her kingdom suffer for forty years because of it. Ceridwen came up out of the dream with the bracelet on her wrist and stayed there.

Page explained it to them while they dressed. The bracelet makes hags. It eats what a hag has not yet eaten. It had been feeding on Coy all night, and if Ceridwen had failed to attune it, the story would have ended badly for everyone in the room.

Downstairs, porridge from Carly Two-Ton and a quiet table. Legion watched the room. A woman with auburn hair was watching them back, with the patience of someone whose head is doing arithmetic somewhere behind her face. She came over when Legion challenged her.

Fiona Firefoot, of the Mercenary Guild. She had heard what they did for Ethan and she wanted help with something bigger: elvish ruins found near town, and a ring she had been sent to find. Coy made her prove her token. She did. Page read her and found a woman who wanted something and had decided not to lie about all of it.

She was not trusted by the town, she said, on account of a vase. She had fallen out with her old party and did not care to explain. The notes she put on the table had been written by a construction worker she had gotten drunk, and Page read enough of the shorthand to see a pawn-shop mark in Thieves' Cant in the corner. The goddess she was hunting wore three stars above a crescent moon, first pantheon, older than the current one.

Simon warned her about the fey work they had been doing. Fiona admitted the ruins would be full of wights, which was more honesty than they were expecting.

They gave her a contract instead of a refusal. The ring to Fiona, unless it proved more than she said. All books to the Bard's College, carried by the Five. Money split even. One item per person, chosen in turn. She signed it while Ceridwen asked questions she did not quite answer.

Page put the entrance under the mayor's house, on the hill that did not look like a hill. They agreed to leave within the hour.
