# 1.12 — The Siege of Lobridge

The soldiers came in from three directions, well-armed and unhurried, against a militia that had been handed whatever the town could find. Legion laid caltrops in the dark and took their true form above the road, and the wasps did work no human-shaped fighter could do. Page made his first sheets of paper into blades and hung the night with them, and learned that the Shrouded walked through them rather than stop.

Coy came over the east bridge as a lion with the militia falling back behind him. On the west road Ceridwen put soldiers to sleep with a charm, climbed the inn roof for a better view, and began feeding strength into Simon and Degnr out of the memories in the bracelet. Simon fought beside Edreth and the magistrate and held the line while the town found its courage.

The caster across the river was the reason the Shrouded kept getting up. Legion found it first, a mouthless thing with black eyes that still managed to smile, and it put a tree between them and Legion by blowing the tree apart. Coy smelled the decay it carried and went for it in a rage that had very old reasons.

Ceridwen's fight went somewhere none of them could follow. The rogue Shrouded tried for the Temple, where the town's families were, and she grabbed it with everything she had and turned it around. The bond she made to do it was real on both ends, and the bracelet's six voices spent themselves holding her out of the deep black water she kept seeing behind it. Twice she nearly went under anyway. She came back both times, and the second time only because the spirits put a light between her and it.

Legion fell. The caster hit them hard enough that the swarm came apart, and Coy arrived one step too late and made it personal. He took the caster's head in his teeth and broke it like a roasted nut. For a moment a glowing collar hung around its neck with a chain of light running off to the south, and then the whole thing burst and the caster was gone.

Simon saw Ceridwen standing frozen on the bridge with the bond still in her and did the only thing that ends a spell like that. He tackled her over the railing and into the river, turning so that his back hit the water first. Page ran past them both to the mayor, who was being held by the last Shrouded, and put a conjured book through its skull.

The mayor's wife came down to the bridge while the fighting stuttered out. Morwenna Greyson did not flinch from a bear, greeted Coy as a druid, and carved bright runes in the air that took pieces off the Shrouded's flesh when nothing else did. She asked, afterward, to speak with Page. He said he would.

Two prisoners survived, both asleep from Ceridwen's charm. The cleanup went late. In the morning the tavern came downstairs to find itself full of cheering people, and Mayor Greyson invited the Five to a party that they politely handed back to the town. Coy and Ceridwen spent the day healing survivors. Simon built a prosthetic leg for a man who had lost his, with a craftsman's patience he had not shown before. Page looked for Edreth and found that he had ridden out in the night, and left word with the local Libertines that the Librarian had come asking.

Coy went through the dead soldiers' pockets and found nothing worth the trouble, except the memory of the coin he had seen on the floor of a watch house. The shark belonged to House Vardon of Raifor.
