# 9 — The Shrouded in the Alley

The Five carried Page into an alley between the workshop walls and got their weapons out, because the men following them were not men. The Shrouded moved too smoothly for that, and they did not run out of blood.

What followed rewrote the rules. In the middle of it the world folded, and the party stood in a hall with a queen's banners on the walls. Lilliana of Tray, ancient, patient, and entirely real, gave them ioun stones that circled their heads and told them the only thing worth knowing about her enemy: the Shrouded remember the flesh when struck with focused will.

Each of them found the trick in their own way. Page's psychic blades cut into the papery soul-stuff under the surface. Coy channeled the stones through his swarm. Simon bonded with his stone and put a yklwa through a Shrouded's chest cleanly enough to end it. Legion reshaped their staff into a conduit and the wasps went inside one and took it apart. Ceridwen, bracelet glowing, fed Lilliana the Tale of the Avenger and erased the last of them with a whisper.

It hurt before it ended. Legion took a blow that cracked their shield. Page halved a hit with a flinch and a step. Lilliana herself took a strike in the gut and needed Coy's hands to stay standing.

When the vision frayed, she looked at Ceridwen's bracelet and named it. A hag-made prison, full of witnesses the hags could not finish eating, and she was one of them. Morgath was another. The Night Doctors and the Shrouded and the hags had been allies since before humanity reached this coast, and if they were moving openly again, one city's problems were no longer the size of the thing.

Her instruction was plain enough to carry out and heavy enough to carry: commune with the bracelet. Use it. That is the chink in their armor. Morgath's promise came after, and it was the other kind of promise. She would teach why power must be feared; he would teach why it must be used.

They came back to the alley with Bill still braying and a crowd of guards and gawkers staring at a group of people who had apparently been fighting nothing at all. The ioun stones were gone. Legion still held a stick.

Page looked at Ceridwen and told her, in pieces, that he did not blame her, and then fell down with a purple light glowing in the wound her magic had put in his chest.
