# 1.5 — Wit and Words

*Oxia 17th, 1396cr · beneath the well, outside Lobridge*



The well's wooden cap crumbled at a touch. Ceridwen found the stolen satyr icon in the grass, followed the way it pointed, and set it into a slot in the stonework. The ground beside the well opened on a staircase.

Coy went first. Legion came last and left crumbs, in case the stairs had opinions about being used. At the bottom waited a cavern with a pool at its center and one flower growing out of it, shimmering in colors that had no business being in one plant. The stairs collapsed behind them. Ceridwen danced clear of the falling stone, which left the party sealed in with whatever lived there.

Laughter came out of the dark, and the Merrymaker stepped into the flower's light. He was tall, shaped like a satyr, unclothed, with ram's horns wearing a crown of blooms. He knew their names. He said they were welcome, and did not say they were free to leave.

Pipifluer played with them for a while. He claimed the thefts had been gifts in disguise, then proved himself a liar and a truth-teller both by waking the lute's enchantment and dropping the party into their happiest memories. Coy and Legion fought clear. Simon lost patience with the games, and Page said out loud that all conversation is manipulation, which the trickster liked enough to keep talking.

It became a contest. Page set a riddle of his own: you can swallow me, but I can consume you. Pipifluer could not answer it and asked for best two of three. His own came next, and Coy and Ceridwen named it at once: a reflection.

Beaten at riddles, he took them to the last game. A song that carried everything they had discussed, and their long chase after him through the festival, all of it.

Ceridwen gave him four verses, sitting in a glade that had not been there a moment before. Wit and words, a dance in a glade. Do you truly love, seek delight, or flee the night? Tears and trinkets and a well that threatened harm, and the singer still standing, hearts shining, whatever he willed.

Pipifluer applauded and gave her the lute.

Then he paid out the truth, because the game was over. The pixies' Garden of Gleeful Delights had been built to imprison something. A god, he said, the same Great Evil Barbello had warned them about. He added that Barbello was untrustworthy, that predatory things hunted beneath mortal perception, and that the Five must bar them from this realm.

He gave the key to Page, the key the pixies had guarded. The glade vanished. The stairs reappeared. As they climbed, Page looked back and watched the pool drain into dry silt. He dug into the desiccated flower bed and retrieved a brass compass etched with elven runes.

Outside, the festival gates were barring entry. They pooled eleven prize tickets for two glass vials of healing draught, one of which Simon set aside for personal reasons. Ceridwen felt a stinging welt form on her palm, skin rising into an ink-red map that indicated the northern road. Page tapped the compass; the needle spun without catching a pole.

They reached Lobridge at sunrise with a new job waiting and a trickster's warnings riding along.
