*Oxia 16th–17th, 1396cr · the Feylight Festival*



With the job agreed, they had hours of festival left and no reason to waste them. The food stalls came first. The stew they chose, Moonfire and earthy, warmed them to the bone and put odd little blessings on the rest of the night; Simon kept the paper bowls. Legion ate alone behind a trash can, where two fey cockroaches were discussing mushroom magic and their chances at a glowbug ball, and skewered them mid-sentence.

Then the buying. Ceridwen found a pixie's paint stall under flowering wisteria and spent an hour learning that the price of good paint is good paint, until Simon and Page talked the pixie into relaxing her exchange policy and Page quietly paid. Simon haggled red and gold cloth down to something he could defend at the family dinner table. Page bought bookbinding supplies and looked happier than anything at the festival had made him yet.

Legion and Coy went to the menagerie. Coy fell in love four times: a rainbow horse called Whir, a pair of blue foxes who gave him a silver bell and let him run with them in their shape for a while, and a hundred other animals, all of them calmer than animals have any business being. Legion looked at the singing spiders and asked practical questions, and got practical answers about an ancient wizard's experiments.

They met outside Eldwyn's Emporium with the night half gone, and made it a few steps toward Kajik's before the pixies hit them. A whole swarm of them, out of their minds. The Key to the Garden of Gleeful Delights had been stolen, and the garden, they said, was what it protected. Ceridwen sang them quiet and got a description out of the noise: a shadow, laughing, gone that way.

Legion found the trail and Page baited it with an illusory copy of the key, and the hunt went through one stolen lute, one robbed merchant, and a child who had seen something move fast. The tracks were hooves. Simon remembered a story from his nursery about a trickster the fey call a Pipifluer, and nobody liked the shape of the thing.

Two half-elves pointed them to an old well outside the stalls, half of it standing past the festival's boundary. That was where the hoofprints stopped.

The festival had two hours left in it.
