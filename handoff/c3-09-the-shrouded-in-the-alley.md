# 9 — The Shrouded in the Alley

*Umbria 17th, 1396cr · an alley in Latesa*



They got Page off the street and into an alley between workshop walls, out of the sight of the guards coming at a run, and stood over him while the purple light in the wound slowly went out.

Then the alley went away, and the party stood somewhere else entirely: a hall with a queen's banners on the walls, and the Shrouded coming at them across it.

Lilliana of Tray had pulled them out of the street into her own hall, and she gave them the means to fight. Ioun stones circled their heads, and she told them the only thing worth knowing about her enemy: the Shrouded remember the flesh when struck with focused will.

Each of them found the trick in their own way. Page's psychic blades cut into the papery soul-stuff under the surface. Coy channeled the stones through his swarm. Simon bonded with his stone and put a yklwa through a Shrouded's chest cleanly enough to end it. Legion reshaped their staff into a conduit and the wasps went inside one and took it apart. Ceridwen, bracelet glowing, fed Lilliana the Tale of the Avenger and erased the last of them with a whisper.

It hurt before it ended. Legion took a blow that cracked their shield. Page halved a hit with a flinch and a step. Lilliana herself took a strike in the gut and needed Coy's hands to stay standing.

When the hall frayed, she looked at Ceridwen's bracelet and named it. A hag-made prison, full of witnesses the hags could not finish eating, and she was one of them. Morgath was another. The Night Doctors and the Shrouded and the hags had been allies since before humanity reached this coast, and if they were moving openly again, one city's problems were no longer the size of the thing.

Her instruction was plain enough to carry out: commune with the bracelet. Use it. That is the chink in their armor. Morgath's promise came after. Lilliana would teach Ceridwen why the power had to be feared. Morgath offered the use of it.

They snapped back into the alley with Bill still braying, the ioun stones gone, Legion holding a plain stick, and a crowd of guards and onlookers staring at a group of people who had spent the last minutes standing very still in an alley doing nothing anyone could see.

Page looked at Ceridwen and told her, in pieces, that he did not blame her. Then he fell down, with a purple light glowing in the wound her magic had put in his chest.
