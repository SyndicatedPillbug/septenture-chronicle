# 18 — Official Business

<!-- source: content/the-chronicle/c3-18-official-business.md | dateline: Umbria, 1396cr · the Basin, Latesa | description: The fight for the witness ends, and the men who started it turn out to be the city's own. -->

*Umbria, 1396cr · the Basin, Latesa*



Nara opened the door upon seeing the blood-soaked jacket. She recognized the cloth and the torn seams, and Ceridwen's grief convinced her to speak. Speaking in whispers, Nara warned that Watch informants watched every street in the Basin, and neighbors who asked questions had vanished without notice. Whatever she had witnessed, someone had threatened her to ensure her silence.

Then the alley outside went sideways. Men came in with clubs and the confidence of muscle backed by the weight of law — and it was law, just not the Watch's. They called Selim a traitor for standing there, and then they made it true by attacking him, and he held the mouth of the alley alone while the party turned the ambush into something harder and uglier than it was meant to be.

Magic cracked the street open. The spellcasters showed what they were. Legion's wasps filled the air until the alley hummed. When it was over the attackers were down, Nara was alive, and the party had a prisoner who was afraid of his own answers. One man got away, which meant somebody would eventually come looking for the rest.

They took their prisoner to the Hive's old sanctum and asked him what he thought he had been doing. The answer made everything worse. The men were city militia, on official work, sent to break up a meeting of Libertine terrorists. Underneath that story sat money, fear, and Vardon's reach — and underneath all of it, one thing that did not fit any of it. One of the dead men carried a faint necromantic wrongness in him, a twist in the soul that spoke of something supernatural having touched him and left its fingerprint.

So the arithmetic stopped adding up in every direction at once. Simon's murder, the missing poor, the frightened witnesses, and the city's official silence were becoming one pattern, and the pattern was starting to look like it wore a V. The Watch's corrupt side would be set on them now. Their one lawful ally was a captain whose protection had limits nobody had tested.

And in the corner of the room, a prisoner sat very still, knowing just enough to be frightened of his own answers.
