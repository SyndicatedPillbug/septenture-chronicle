# 23 — Something Else Is Coming

*1396cr · the old Vahael cistern, the Whites*



The party had just put the brood-thieves down when the floor of the cistern opened. A pillar of rats, a worm made of many small bodies, came up under the last thug and took him screaming into the earth, and left a ragged hole and a silence behind it.

Coy, still in the bear, nearly lunged at the rats and stopped himself. Page threw a spectral hand and shoved Ceridwen off the lip of the hole. Rats do not climb in a column. Whatever had called them registered, to him, as kin. Legion's thought was plain: something is very wrong here. Selim got up on the dry stones with his sword out.

Coy cast and found the whole of the Vahael grounds humming with the same transmutation current they had felt on arrival, sharper now, and touched by something he could only call divine. He knew of no god of rats. Page searched his memory for a fallen god of beasts and found only the God War and its thousands of small dead divinities, and no name that fit.

Then the violet light began to pulse in the dome, and Hive moved toward it. Through the pheromone link, Legion felt what Hive felt. A call. For so long it had understood so little, and then Legion had come, many-like-us-yet-single, and now it understood why it had been brought to this place. Page and Ceridwen called warnings, and it was already too late for warnings. Hive rose, poured out an amber sheen to answer the violet, and the two colors condensed to a single burning point and burst.

Where the swarm had been stood a silhouette of a person, hard to look at, visible only from the corner of the eye. For a handful of seconds Legion could still see the wasp bodies moving inside it. Then even that went, and Hive tore up through the glass-and-iron roof and was gone into the sky, and its answer drifted back down: I can't. Something else is coming. I have to meet it, or you'll all be destroyed.

Then the shouting started, and the armored feet, and the dogs, a minute out at most. They took the sluice tunnel rather than the wall, Page molding earth behind them and laying the smell of roasting meat over their tracks to pull the dogs astray, Coy washing the trail out of the stone. In the dark his Disguise Self fell away, and the blonde stranger became Page again, and something small and precious went missing from his person without his noticing.

The black-clad militia came into the cistern behind them in trios — sword, polearm, firearm — moving like hunters, with folded tripods at their belts and three leashed dogs. The party came up out of the far end of the sluice outside the Whites, near the docks, with the argument still unfinished: two unconscious thugs left behind, one carried, and Ceridwen one moment too slow with her apology to the one they could not take, which was the exact moment the guards saw her face.

They ran through grey dawn fog, turned into a quiet courtyard with a star of brickwork at its center, and found a figure standing in the middle of it. Small, slight, wrapped in a long coat of red and purple, wide-brimmed hat, two red burning eyes, a spear that folded into two curved pistols, and a knee-high contraption of sticks and metal clicking at its feet.

It fired, and the lead swordsman of the three militiamen coming up the alley behind them dropped, and a second shot nicked Coy's ear.
